THE ORIGIN
STORY
01
CREATOR
✏️

Chester Gould

Born June 20, 1900 in Pawnee, Oklahoma. After years of rejection from the Chicago Tribune, Gould's 10th submission — a square-jawed detective — finally landed in October 1931.

02
THE PITCH

Plainclothes Tracy

The strip began as "Plainclothes Tracy," renamed Dick Tracy before launch. Gould wanted a comic that directly confronted gangster culture — inspired by Al Capone's stranglehold on Chicago.

"I'll give the public a detective who fights back — no more milk-and-water stuff. This cop shoots first and asks questions later."
— Chester Gould, 1931
03
WHO IS HE?

Dick
Tracy

A plainclothes police detective in an unnamed American city. Incorruptible, tenacious, and endlessly inventive. Tracy set the template for every tough-cop procedural that followed.

Chapter 02
ROGUES
GALLERY
🎩
FLATTOP
ALIAS: FLATTOP JONES SR.

Hired hitman with a notoriously flat cranium. Became so popular readers wrote in to keep him alive. Gould killed him anyway — on a crowded subway.

😷
PRUNEFACE
ALIAS: BOCHE

Wartime saboteur with a grotesquely wrinkled face. One of the strip's most chilling wartime villains, inspired by WWII anxieties about enemy agents.

🎭
THE BLANK
ALIAS: FRANK REDRUM

No face — literally. Wore a featureless flesh-colored mask concealing their true identity. One of the strip's most surreal and unsettling visual concepts.

🧊
ICEBOX
ALIAS: ICEBOX JONES

A cold-blooded killer as calculating as his name suggests. His methodical approach to crime made him one of Tracy's most dangerous foes.

🦷
BIG BOY
ALIAS: ALPHONSE BIG BOY CAPRICE

The original Dick Tracy villain — a Capone-inspired mob boss whose murder of Tracy's future father-in-law launched the entire strip.

🎪
MUMBLES
ALIAS: MUMBLES MCGURK

Spoke in an incomprehensible mumble, making interrogation comically impossible. Later adapted by Warren Beatty in the 1990 film, played by Dustin Hoffman.

Chapter 03
THE WRIST
COMMUNICATOR
2-WAY WRIST TV · PATENT PENDING
  • 📅

    Introduced in 1946

    Chester Gould introduced the 2-Way Wrist Radio in January 1946 — the smartphone concept 61 years before the iPhone launched.

  • 📺

    Upgraded to Wrist TV — 1964

    Gould evolved the technology to include video calling, predicting FaceTime and video conferencing by nearly half a century.

  • 🛰️

    Wrist Computer — 1980s

    The device evolved again into a wrist-mounted computer, anticipating the Apple Watch and smartwatch revolution.

  • 💡

    Cited by Inventors

    Engineers at Motorola, Apple, and Samsung have cited Dick Tracy's wrist communicator as a direct inspiration for their wearable technology.

  • ⭐

    Cultural Icon

    The watch is considered one of the most prophetic inventions in pop culture history — a piece of fiction that literally shaped the future.

Chapter 04
90 YEARS
OF JUSTICE
1931
The Strip Debuts
Dick Tracy premieres in the Detroit Mirror and New York Daily News on October 4. Big Boy commits his first crime. America gets its detective.
1937
Radio Comes Calling
Dick Tracy hits the airwaves. The radio serial runs for a decade, bringing the detective's voice to millions of American households.
1945
Flattop's Reign & Demise
Flattop Jones becomes the strip's most popular villain. Reader mail begs Gould to spare him. Gould drowns him anyway. Moral of the story: crime still doesn't pay.
1946
The Wrist Radio Appears
Gould introduces the 2-Way Wrist Radio — the most prophetic gadget in comics history. Engineers and inventors would cite it for generations.
1967
Chester Gould Steps Back
After nearly four decades as sole creator, Gould begins transitioning responsibilities. His legacy: the definitive American detective comic and the language of crime noir.
1990
Warren Beatty's Film
Warren Beatty produces, directs, and stars in the blockbuster Dick Tracy film. Madonna, Al Pacino, and Dustin Hoffman join. A visual masterpiece of primary colors and comic-panel cinematography.
2011
80th Anniversary
Dick Tracy marks eight decades of publication — one of the longest-running American comic strips in history. New storylines, classic characters, same yellow hat.
TODAY
Still on the Case
Dick Tracy continues to run in newspapers and online. The strip that launched crime comics, predicted the smartphone, and gave us the square jaw of justice still publishes — every single day.
Chapter 05
THE MEN
BEHIND THE BADGE
CG
Creator · Writer · Artist
Chester Gould

Born in Pawnee, Oklahoma in 1900, Chester Gould spent years submitting strip ideas to the Chicago Tribune before hitting gold. He wrote and drew Dick Tracy from 1931 to 1977 — 46 consecutive years — creating over 100 villains, inventing the wrist communicator, and defining the visual language of crime comics. Gould's bold black-ink style, influenced by German Expressionism, made Dick Tracy instantly recognizable and infinitely imitated. He passed away in 1985, leaving behind one of the most enduring characters in American popular culture.

MC
Current Creative Team Lead
Mike Curtis & Joe Staton

Writer Mike Curtis and artist Joe Staton have steered Dick Tracy since 2011, bringing a deep respect for Gould's legacy alongside fresh storylines that honor nine decades of history. Staton's artwork channels Gould's bold lines while adding his own dynamic energy to chase scenes and confrontations. Under their stewardship, the strip has introduced modern technology — including actual smartphones — while never losing the timeless yellow-coat silhouette that defines Dick Tracy for every generation of reader.

Dick Tracy moves to television with Ralph Byrd reprising his film role, bringing the yellow-hat detective to the new medium of American broadcast TV.

Multiple video game adaptations across NES, Sega Genesis, and Game Boy introduced Dick Tracy to a new generation of players in the wake of Beatty's blockbuster film.

Chester Gould inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Industry Award Hall of Fame. Dick Tracy recognized as a foundational work of American sequential art.
